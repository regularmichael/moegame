Platform: Steam
Genre: Survival horror, science fiction, body horror, modern setting, semi open world
Difficult: Medium to hard

Brief Synopsis:
Evil Within 2 is a survival horror game that rewards player adaptation and curiosity. As Sebastian Castellanos, you enter STEM, a sort of virtual world, to save your daughter that you believe died years ago. 

**SETTING**
The primary setting is Union, a sort of American "everytown" that has been corrupted, turning its citizens into horrible monsters. The streets are an uncanny mix of dream-like surrealism and Disneyesque perfectionism, and as the game goes on the town becomes even strangers. 

Additionally, several antagonists have turned Union into their playground, giving themselves immense power and unique environments in the game. As you progress through the town in a somewhat open-world fashion, the world changes in response to the state of the narrative. There are also surreal "tunnels" that can be used to help Sebastian Castellanos reach different parts of the city as they become disconnected from each other.

Occasionally, players find a mirror that takes them to a safe hub where they can save their game, upgrade their equipment, and review some files. There are also some characters here that can be talked to, namely Kidman, who I talk about below. This area is lore-rich, true to Sebastian's character, and a safe breather from the dangers.

There are several larger locations that act as major setpieces, such as a movie theatre or a hospital. While this is probably a better setup for a potential second game, there's still a lot that can be pulled for the first game (Ridgewreath Farm):
1. The changing city is a good model for how the magical pockets affect the reality in the game
2. A safe area where players can save the game, review game lore, and learn more about the character

**CHARACTERS**
Players control Sebastian Castellanos, a down-and-out former police officer desperately looking for the organization that ruined his life in the first Evil Within game. Instead of finding them, they come to him, needing his help to rescue his daughter and save their precious STEM project. Sebastian has a practical, no-nonsense approach that is a decent template for Moe. However, I envision her to be a bit more empathetic and investigative than he is shown to be in this game.

The supporting cast include the Mobius team, who were sent in before Sebastian to help get things under control. The player can be introduced to them through artifacts in the safe hub before they meet the characters. Some of them have uncertain fates, others become enemies later, and others support Sebastian throughout the entire game. The range that they provide is very cool, and while I don't plan to have this number of supporting characters in the first game, this is again a good template for a possible second game. 

In Sebastian's safe hub, he can talk to two characters, both of whom return from the first game. The first is Tatiana, who is a nurse that provides upgrades for the character. Tatiana is cryptic and mysterious, often providing nothing more than just a creepy discussion. She's interesting, but I don't fancy a character like that for this series.

The second is Kidman, who is guiding Sebastian from outside of STEM. As players collect files in the game, they may come across slides that they can use at a slide projector in the office. When they do, Sebastian and Kidman will have very frank conversations that provide more of their own backstories, give them emotional resolution, and tie up some other threads. This is a really cool thing, and I would like to use Kidman's role as a model for Barry, even though I don't plan to have him appear in the game. The way Moe can talk to him in a safe area - just by making a call, to help make players more aware of what's going on - is a really nice touch. 

The antagonist characters range quite a bit, with the most striking being a serial killer obsessed with the artistic display of his murders. He is creepy and his sections are some of the strongest surreal horror moments, but he is dealt with about halfway through the game. The second antagonist is a priest obsessed with fire - I feel like he doesn't make much of an impact, but he provides decent back story. The final antagonist is Sebastian's own wife, who is there to help anchor STEM. Her defeat is necessary, but I don't remember why. I just remember that it's extremely effective emotional storytelling, and that is something that I really want to incorporate into these games.

**COMBAT**
Combat is third-person, over-the-shoulder gunplay. Typical combat for this style of game - gunplay is fairly meaty, but usually standard. Nothing really to pull from this game that isn't already covered by other games, and I'm still on the fence about the inclusion of guns in the game, so there's not really anything in terms of direct combat to talk about here. 

There are some melee elements, but they aren't that important either. There are axes that can do a lot of damage but break, and a permanent knife that does very little damage. There are sneak attacks that can be done that kill an unsuspecting enemy, which is an interesting idea that we can expand on for our combat system re: magic and releasing souls. There are also distraction items like bricks and bottles - a useful inclusion.

Characters can craft combat things, either at a bench or in the field (which requires more than at a bench). The crafting is pretty basic, nothing to really note here other than it's a good template. Typical resource get -> make item loop. 

**TAKEAWAY**
Evil Within 2 is a really interesting horror game. The open world, surreal presentation, and emotional storytelling are the primary things to pull from this game. I'll be playing more of it to expand on this.
