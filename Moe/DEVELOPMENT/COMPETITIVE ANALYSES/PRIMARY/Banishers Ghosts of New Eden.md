Platform: Steam
Genre: Fantasy, mystery, action RPG, supernatural
Difficult: Medium

**Brief Synopsis:**
Characters play Red mac Raith, a banisher, and Antea Duarte, his deceased lover, as they work to rid New Eden of a variety of courses. As they play the game, they can make choices on how to deal with their situations. This will impact the remainder of the game. The overarching choice of the game - to bring Antea back at the cost of human lives, or to allow her to ascend peacefully while leaving Red alone - is a major thread. 

**SETTING**
The setting is New World-era America, before the establishment of the 13 original colonies as a single nation. The particular setting isn't useful to this game, but the gloomy setting and focus on nature and mystical aspects of the land could be useful to our story. I'll be keeping an eye on how this develops and if it can be improved on by our designs. 

**CHARACTERS**
Players control both Red mac Raith and Antea Duarte, swapping between the two at will, although the game defaults to Red for most of the time. 

Red is idealistic and emotional, while Antea is reserved and pragmatic. Their dynamic as a couple is a primary point of the game, and the decision the player makes about major cases can shape how their relationship develops.

**NARRATIVE**
The narrative is explored through character interaction and notes found in the world. The narrative has elements of choice, although the degree to which they make a difference outside of Antea's ascension or revival is unclear to me at 6 hours in. Never the less, some choices are given to players, which includes a potential for choosing a response. This is a hub-dialogue system, although sometimes the response you choose in the final option you can have the conversation. I'm not sure what purpose this serves other than to be a realistic depiction of conversations, but I do like it.

Most NPCs can be interacted with it seems, and each provide answers to questions the characters would logically have about the events in the game. The context provided is useful every time and the NPCs are distinct enough to leave an impression, even if you may not outright recall their names or purpose in the village off the top of your hand. This is more important for the second game, I think. However, I think it would also be a good template to create memorable victims whose history is imparted to Moe through psychometry, magic, or files.

The important thing that would be useful to use here is the investigation aspect. When investigating, players are given some things to investigate, and you know which ones using UI indicators or particle effects surrounding points of interest. Red and Antea both have unique ways to contribute to an investigation. I think the pursuit of facts needed to solve the case are interesting, but they are laid out very clearly, without much room for error or ambiguity and definitive endings to each case. I would like to make this a bit more uncertain or have multiple options for the players of the Moe game. 

There is an overarching narrative, but at least in the early game it takes a back seat to getting to know the world and establish a baseline for the Antea/Red relationship. I think it works for this game, and it could be useful for a second game in this series. For Ridgewreath Farm, however, the narrative should be laser-focused on the McCall's and how Moe plans to solve the case there, rather than establishing a relationship between her and another character. If any relationships are explored, it should be the ones between the McCalls, the Priest character and Annamarie McCall, and relationships between the victims of the farm. 

**CHARACTER PROGRESSION**
Character progression is a standard RPG format. The characters gain experience by resolving questions, moving the narrative forward, and defeating enemies in combat. This experience points contribute to an overall level, which give Red a skill point to spend on his exclusive abilities. Antea's skill points are earned through other means and can be used on her specific abilities. Progression on one character necessitates progression on another. It's a cool approach, but probably not useful here.

Equipment can be improved using resources gained from the world. This encourages exploration and combat, as a mix of natural resources and rewards from defeating enemies are used here. Probably not exactly useful for Ridgewreath Farm, but I'd be open to exploring it as potential way to increase magical effectiveness. We need to establish a basic gameplay prototype, figure out magic, and determine the finer aspects of combat before we make this decision, though. Either way, the important here is that components are useful.

Characters can also increase attributes by clearing out ghost infestations or finding altars in the world. These provide either a general boost (i.e. +1 to all stats) or a larger boost to one stat (i.e. +10 to health). I don't intend to use stats for this game, but the idea of finding a boost in the environment is a great way to reward player exploration and resourcefulness while giving us a cool way to add environmental storytelling. 

**ENEMY DESIGN**
Enemies at this point are a mix of standard animals, "ghost" types, and  "possessed" types. Standard animals, thusfar, have just been wolves, which act.. like wolves. Each other type of enemy is best dealt with by a specific character (see below). Ghost enemies are wispy and mostly aesthetically distinguished by different colors to indicate what they do - for example, green enemies are ranged, while blue enemies are fast and agile. They're mostly unremarkable, but I'll come back to this once I have more experience and can find a way to draw inspiration from their design, as I do think there's something here.

Possessed enemies have a lot more physical diversity, based on skeletons or corpses found in the world. A dog skeleton gives the enemy dog-like behaviors, in addition to some properties based on whatever spirits possessed them. Corpses strewn about the battlefield are able to be possessed, and the player is alerted if a ghost tries to possess one. If it happens, players will need to beat the spirit out of the corpse, then kill the spirit. It's a cool system, and having to keep an eye on enemy behaviors and responding to their moves is definitely something I want to incorporate here.

One thing I think we can do better here, if it's not included in Banishers at a later state, is if enemies that died during combat can be possessed or reused somehow. Knowing that this is a possibility will keep players on edge and be more considerate of how they approach combat, if they want to do it at all. 

**EXPLORATION**
Exploration is interesting, and can be a useful prototype for Psychometry. Players are given items in the world, which provide clues or context for events in the world. As they explore, a red or blue sigil will flash on the players hand. A red sigil requires Red to conduct a ritual, while a blue sigil requires Antea to manipulate spiritual residue in the world. 

Red's rituals require items found in the world, and they are roughly broken up into 3 types: call a spirit, get a vision, or summon malevolent force. The spirit and vision elements specifically allow the player to interact with spectral recreations of events or characters in the world. This is an interesting, if basic idea, and I think we can use this as a template to expand on Moe's spellcasting abilities and Psychometric visions.

Antea, when needing to manipulate spiritual residue, will see lingering spiritual threads and gain insight into certain curses, hauntings, or other supernatural forces in the world. This is a good way to include Psychometry, we just need to make it more potent and also more dangerous. 

**COMBAT**
Players have a decent range of combat options. At first, players rely on melee attacks, although ranged options (such as rifles) appear to unlock later. Attacks generally fall into light/heavy attacks, each with a range of combos that can be unlocked as players progress in the game.

A unique thing about this game is the dual Red/Antea combat options. Players can swap between the two so long as Antea has enough spirit energy to use. Everything Antea does in combat requires spirit energy, which is generated by Red's regular attacks. Antea is best used against "possessed" enemies. There are many skills that require swapping between the two characters in order to be effective.

I will be coming back to this, as I'm pretty early in the game.

**TAKEAWAY**
Banishers is an interesting game, if not a totally unique one, or one that is 1:1 important for the development of Ridgewreath Farm. However, I do think there are a lot of really cool ideas that we can borrow here:
1. The rituals that need to be performed and how they use resources. Knowing which one to use is incredibly cool, we just need to make it more risk-reward compliant and less railroaded.
2. The investigation aspects are cool, even if they are straightforward.
